Where to Find Pet Sitters & Walkers in Guangzhou

Home / Travel Blog / Blog Details

Guangzhou, a sprawling metropolis where ancient Lingnan culture meets futuristic skyscrapers, is a city that never sleeps—and neither do its pet owners. Whether you’re a digital nomad exploring the city’s vibrant street food scene in Liwan District, a business traveler attending the Canton Fair, or a local resident planning a weekend getaway to the Baiyun Mountain trails, finding reliable pet sitters and walkers in Guangzhou can feel like navigating a maze of Cantonese alleyways. But fear not: this guide will take you through the best resources, apps, local hotspots, and insider tips to ensure your furry friend gets the care they deserve while you soak up the city’s unique blend of old and new.

Why Guangzhou’s Pet Care Scene Is Booming

Guangzhou isn’t just the third-largest city in China; it’s also a trendsetter in the country’s pet industry. With over 100 million pet dogs and cats nationwide, China’s pet economy is skyrocketing, and Guangzhou is at the forefront. The city’s pet owners are increasingly treating their animals like family members, leading to a surge in demand for professional pet sitting and dog walking services. From expat-heavy neighborhoods like Zhujiang New Town to more traditional areas like Haizhu District, the need for trustworthy caregivers has never been higher. Add to that the rise of “pet-friendly” tourism—where travelers bring their dogs to parks, cafes, and even shopping malls—and you have a recipe for a thriving pet service ecosystem.

The Digital Goldmine: Apps and Platforms for Finding Pet Sitters

1. PetBacker: The Global Giant with a Local Twist

If you’ve ever used Rover or Wag in the US, PetBacker is the Chinese equivalent—but with a stronger foothold in Guangzhou. This app connects pet owners with sitters, walkers, and boarders, offering features like GPS-tracked walks, real-time photo updates, and in-app messaging. The platform is popular among Guangzhou’s expat community, and many sitters are bilingual (Mandarin and English), making it a breeze for international travelers. Simply download the app, search for “Guangzhou,” and filter by services like “dog walking” or “pet sitting.” Prices range from 80 to 150 RMB per walk, depending on the duration and your dog’s size. Pro tip: Look for sitters with “verified” badges and high ratings—local pet parents often leave detailed reviews in both Chinese and English.

2. Xiaoxiang Pet (小象宠物): The Local Favorite

For a more localized experience, Xiaoxiang Pet is a Chinese app that’s gaining traction in Guangzhou. It’s similar to PetBacker but tends to have lower prices (around 50–100 RMB per walk) and a stronger presence in residential communities like Tianhe and Panyu. The app’s interface is primarily in Mandarin, so you might need to use a translation tool or ask a local friend for help. However, the sheer number of sitters available—many of whom are students or part-time pet lovers—means you’ll often find same-day bookings, even during peak travel seasons like Chinese New Year or National Day Golden Week.

3. WeChat Mini-Programs: The Hidden Gem

You can’t talk about Guangzhou without mentioning WeChat. This super-app isn’t just for messaging; it’s a hub for pet services through mini-programs. Search for terms like “宠物寄养” (pet boarding) or “遛狗” (dog walking) within WeChat, and you’ll find dozens of local providers. Some popular mini-programs include “爱宠之家” (Love Pet Home) and “宠物保姆” (Pet Nanny). These are often run by small businesses or individual sitters who post daily availability. The advantage? You can pay via WeChat Pay, communicate directly, and even share your real-time location. The downside? Quality varies wildly, so always ask for references or video calls before booking.

Brick-and-Mortar Pet Centers: Where to Walk In and Walk Out

Pet Shops with Sitting Services

Guangzhou’s pet shops are more than just retail spaces—many double as daycare and boarding facilities. Head to the Fangcun Pet Market (芳村花鸟鱼虫市场) in Liwan District, one of the largest pet markets in southern China. While it’s famous for selling everything from fish to rabbits, several shops here offer day sitting and walking services for dogs. Prices are negotiable, and you can often leave your pet for a few hours while you explore the market’s chaotic yet fascinating alleys. Just be prepared for a sensory overload of barking, chirping, and the smell of dried fish.

Veterinary Clinics with Added Services

Many vet clinics in Guangzhou now offer pet sitting and walking as an add-on. For example, PetCare Animal Hospital in Tianhe District provides a “Daycare Plus” package that includes walks, feeding, and playtime. This is a great option if your pet has medical needs or if you want the peace of mind that comes with professional veterinary oversight. Rates are higher (around 200–300 RMB per day), but you’re paying for expertise. Plus, most clinics have English-speaking staff, which is a lifesaver for tourists who don’t speak Cantonese or Mandarin.

Neighborhood Hotspots: Where Pet Sitters Hang Out

Zhujiang New Town (珠江新城): The Expat Epicenter

If you’re staying in Zhujiang New Town, you’re in luck. This glitzy district is home to countless pet-friendly cafes, parks, and walking trails. The Guangzhou Opera House area and Huacheng Square are popular spots for morning walks, and you’ll often see professional walkers with groups of dogs—mostly Golden Retrievers, Corgis, and French Bulldogs. To find a sitter here, simply strike up a conversation with other dog owners. The expat community is tight-knit, and many post recommendations on Facebook groups like “Guangzhou Expats” or “Pets in Guangzhou.” You can also check out The Strand, a pet-friendly mall with a dedicated dog park on its rooftop.

Baiyun Mountain (白云山): The Nature Escape

For travelers who want to hike Baiyun Mountain with their dogs but need a sitter for the steep parts, the base of the mountain is a goldmine. Local sitters often wait near the South Gate entrance, offering to walk dogs on the lower trails while you tackle the summit. These are informal arrangements—cash only, and prices are usually 50–80 RMB per hour. The sitters are typically retirees or students who know the area well. Just make sure your dog is comfortable with strangers and has a sturdy leash, as the trails can get crowded on weekends.

Shamian Island (沙面岛): The Colonial Charm

Shamian Island, with its tree-lined avenues and colonial architecture, is a favorite for pet owners who want a relaxed, pedestrian-friendly experience. The island is car-free, making it ideal for off-leash walks (though technically not allowed, locals often ignore the rules). Pet sitters here are a mix of young creatives and elderly residents who treat dog walking as a side hustle. You can find them near the Swan Lake area or the Catholic Church. Again, word-of-mouth is key—ask at nearby cafes like Lian’s Coffee, which is known for its pet-friendly policy.

The Gig Economy: Freelancers and Students

University Students: Affordable and Enthusiastic

Guangzhou is a university city, with institutions like Sun Yat-sen University and South China University of Technology attracting thousands of students. Many of them offer pet sitting and walking services as a way to earn extra cash. You can find them on platforms like Douban (a Chinese social network) or through campus bulletin boards. The rates are low (30–60 RMB per walk), but reliability can be hit or miss. Always meet the student in a public place first, and consider setting up a trial walk before committing to a longer arrangement.

WeChat Groups: The Community Connection

WeChat groups are the backbone of Guangzhou’s pet community. Search for groups like “广州宠物互助” (Guangzhou Pet Mutual Aid) or “狗狗去哪儿” (Where to Go with Dogs) by asking friends or posting in expat forums. These groups are hyper-local, often focusing on specific districts like Tianhe, Haizhu, or Panyu. Members regularly post requests for sitters, and you’ll find everything from last-minute walkers to long-term boarders. The best part? You can see the sitter’s history through group chats and shared photos.

What to Look for in a Pet Sitter or Walker

Credentials and Insurance

While Guangzhou doesn’t have a formal licensing system for pet sitters, many professionals carry pet first-aid certifications or have worked in veterinary clinics. Ask for proof of training, and if possible, choose sitters who offer insurance coverage for accidents. Some platforms like PetBacker include basic insurance in their booking fees.

Communication Skills

Language can be a barrier, especially if you’re a non-Mandarin speaker. Look for sitters who list English as a skill, or use translation apps like Pleco to facilitate conversations. Clear communication about your pet’s routine, dietary restrictions, and medical needs is non-negotiable.

Meet-and-Greet Sessions

Never skip the meet-and-greet. Arrange to meet the sitter at a neutral location, like a nearby park, and observe how they interact with your pet. Do they seem patient? Are they comfortable handling a leash? Do they ask questions about your dog’s behavior? Trust your gut—if something feels off, move on.

The Legal and Cultural Landscape

Leash Laws and Public Behavior

Guangzhou has strict leash laws in most public areas, and fines can range from 50 to 500 RMB for off-leash dogs. Make sure your sitter is aware of these rules, especially in crowded places like metro stations or shopping malls. Also, note that some breeds (like Pit Bulls and Tibetan Mastiffs) are banned in certain districts, so check local regulations before hiring a sitter.

Cultural Attitudes Toward Pets

While Guangzhou is more pet-friendly than many Chinese cities, not everyone is a dog lover. Some elderly residents may be wary of large dogs, and certain parks have designated “no pet” zones. A good sitter will know how to navigate these social nuances, keeping your pet away from sensitive areas and cleaning up after them diligently.

Seasonal Considerations: When to Book in Advance

Chinese New Year: The Pet Care Crunch

During Chinese New Year, Guangzhou’s pet care industry goes into overdrive. Many sitters travel home to their hometowns, leaving a shortage of available workers. Book at least two weeks in advance, and expect prices to double. Some pet hotels, like Paw Paradise in Panyu, offer special holiday packages with festive treats and photo sessions.

National Day Golden Week: The Travel Rush

The first week of October is another peak season. Guangzhou’s parks are packed, and sitters are in high demand. If you’re traveling during this period, consider using a pet boarding service instead of daily walks, as traffic and crowds can make walking stressful for both the sitter and your pet.

Final Tips for a Stress-Free Experience

  • Always carry a copy of your pet’s vaccination records. Some sitters and boarding facilities require proof of rabies and distemper shots.
  • Use GPS trackers for peace of mind. Devices like the Fi Smart Collar or Apple AirTag can help you monitor your pet’s location during walks.
  • Tip generously. While tipping isn’t customary in China, a small bonus (10–20 RMB) for exceptional service is appreciated and ensures you’ll be remembered for future bookings.
  • Leave a detailed note. Write down your pet’s feeding schedule, favorite toys, and any quirks (like a fear of thunderstorms or a love for belly rubs) in both English and Mandarin. Your sitter will thank you.

Guangzhou is a city of contrasts—where neon-lit skyscrapers cast shadows on ancient temples, and where the scent of dim sum mingles with the aroma of street-side barbecue. For pet owners, it’s a place of endless possibilities, but only if you have the right support system. Whether you choose a tech-savvy app, a local student, or a neighborhood veteran, the key is to start early, communicate clearly, and embrace the chaos. After all, in a city that never stops moving, your pet deserves a walker who can keep up.
