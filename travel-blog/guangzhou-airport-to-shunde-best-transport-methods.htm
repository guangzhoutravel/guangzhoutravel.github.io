Guangzhou Airport to Shunde: Best Transport Methods

Home / Travel Blog / Blog Details

Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport (CAN) is one of the busiest aviation hubs in Southern China, serving millions of travelers every year. While many visitors rush directly into Guangzhou city center, an increasing number of savvy tourists and business travelers are heading straight to Shunde, a district of Foshan that has become a culinary and cultural hotspot in its own right. Shunde is renowned as the UNESCO City of Gastronomy, home to some of the most delicate Cantonese dishes, ancient water towns, and a slower, more authentic pace of life compared to the neon chaos of Guangzhou. But getting from the airport to Shunde can be confusing if you don’t know your options. This guide breaks down every major transport method, from the fastest to the most budget-friendly, so you can choose what fits your travel style.

Why Shunde Deserves a Spot on Your Itinerary

Before diving into logistics, it’s worth understanding why Shunde has become a magnet for food lovers and culture seekers. Located about 60 kilometers south of Guangzhou Airport, Shunde was historically a wealthy agricultural and silk-producing region. Today, it is famous for dishes like Shunde raw fish (sashimi-style freshwater fish), double-skin milk (a silky dessert made from buffalo milk), and stuffed lotus root. Beyond food, you can explore the Qinghui Garden, one of the four famous gardens of Guangdong, or wander through the Fengjian Water Village, where canals and ancient bridges rival those of Suzhou. The area is also a hub for furniture shopping, with massive showrooms that attract interior designers from across Asia. All of this means that getting to Shunde efficiently is not just a transport question—it is the first step in unlocking a truly unique travel experience.

Option 1: The Airport Express Bus – The Balanced Choice

For most travelers, the airport express bus is the sweet spot between cost and convenience. Unlike trains or taxis, the bus offers a direct route without requiring transfers at crowded railway stations.

How It Works

The Guangzhou Airport operates several bus lines that connect to Shunde. The most popular is the Airport Bus Line 7, which runs from the arrival hall to Shunde’s Daliang Bus Station and also stops at the Shunde New World Hotel in the city center. Buses depart every 30 to 40 minutes during peak hours, with slightly reduced frequency late at night.

Travel Time and Cost

The journey takes approximately 1 hour and 15 minutes under normal traffic conditions. However, be aware that the highway between Guangzhou and Shunde can get congested during rush hours (7:30–9:00 AM and 5:00–7:00 PM), which can stretch the trip to 1.5 or even 2 hours. The fare is around 60 to 80 RMB per person, making it the most cost-effective option for solo travelers or small groups.

Pros and Cons

Pros: No need to navigate train stations; luggage is stored underneath the bus; direct drop-off at central locations in Shunde. Cons: Traffic can be unpredictable; buses are not as frequent late at night (last departure around 10:30 PM); if your hotel is outside the city center, you may need a short taxi ride from the bus stop.

Pro Tip

Buy your ticket at the official counter inside the arrival hall. Avoid touts who approach you outside—they often sell overpriced tickets or try to steer you into shared vans. The official bus is clearly marked with “Shunde” in English and Chinese (顺德).

Option 2: High-Speed Rail via Guangzhou South Station – The Fastest Route

If speed is your priority, the high-speed rail (HSR) option is unbeatable. China’s bullet trains are punctual, comfortable, and remarkably affordable by international standards. However, this method requires a transfer from the airport to the train station, which adds a layer of complexity.

Step 1: Airport to Guangzhou South Station

You have two ways to reach Guangzhou South Railway Station from the airport: - Metro Line 3: Take the airport metro (Line 3) from the airport to Tiyu Xilu Station, then transfer to Line 2 heading south to Guangzhou South. The entire metro ride takes about 50 minutes and costs around 10 RMB. - Airport Express Bus: There is a dedicated bus route (Airport Bus Line 3) that runs directly to Guangzhou South Station. The bus takes about 45 minutes and costs 20 RMB. This is easier if you have heavy luggage.

Step 2: Guangzhou South to Shunde

From Guangzhou South, high-speed trains depart frequently to Shunde Station (顺德站) or Ronggui Station (容桂站). The journey is astonishingly short—just 8 to 15 minutes depending on the specific stop. Tickets cost between 20 and 30 RMB for a second-class seat. Trains run from early morning (around 6:30 AM) until late evening (around 10:00 PM).

Total Time and Cost

From airport arrival to Shunde city center, expect around 1.5 to 2 hours total, including transfer time. The combined cost (metro + train) is about 40 to 50 RMB, which is actually cheaper than the express bus if you are traveling alone.

Pros and Cons

Pros: Extremely fast train ride; trains are clean and have restrooms; reliable schedule unaffected by road traffic. Cons: Requires a transfer at a busy train station; metro can be crowded with luggage; Shunde Station is not in the city center—you will need a taxi or bus to reach your final destination (about 15–20 minutes by taxi).

Pro Tip

Use the official China Railway app or Alipay’s train booking feature to buy your ticket in advance. During Chinese holidays (like National Day or Spring Festival), high-speed rail tickets to Shunde sell out quickly. Also, note that Shunde has two train stations: Shunde Station (closer to the old town) and Ronggui Station (closer to the furniture district). Choose based on your hotel location.

Option 3: Private Car or Didi – The Premium Convenience

For travelers who value door-to-door service, especially those with large groups, heavy luggage, or late-night arrivals, hiring a private car or using the ride-hailing app Didi is the most stress-free option.

How It Works

You can book a private transfer in advance through platforms like Klook, Trip.com, or directly with local car services. Alternatively, upon exiting the arrival hall, you can open the Didi app (which works with international credit cards if set up properly) and request a ride to your Shunde hotel. The pickup point for Didi is usually at the designated ride-hailing zone on the P1 or P2 parking levels.

Travel Time and Cost

The drive from Guangzhou Airport to central Shunde (e.g., Daliang area) is about 60 to 70 kilometers. Under normal conditions, it takes 50 minutes to 1 hour. The cost ranges from 200 to 350 RMB for a standard sedan, depending on the time of day and demand. During peak hours or rainy weather, surge pricing can push the fare higher.

Pros and Cons

Pros: No transfers; no waiting for schedules; comfortable and private; ideal for families or business travelers. Cons: Most expensive option; traffic can negate the time advantage; communication with the driver may be challenging if you don’t speak Chinese (though Didi has an auto-translate feature).

Pro Tip

If you use Didi, make sure your phone has a local SIM card or an eSIM with data. Airport Wi-Fi is available but often requires SMS verification, which can be tricky for international visitors. Also, save your hotel’s name in Chinese characters (e.g., 顺德大良喜来登酒店) to show the driver. Many drivers do not read English well.

Option 4: Taxi – The Old Reliable (With Caveats)

Taxis are available 24/7 at the airport, and they are a straightforward option if you prefer not to deal with apps. However, this is generally the least recommended method due to cost and potential scams.

How It Works

Follow the signs to the official taxi queue outside the arrival hall. A dispatcher will assign you a car. Always insist on using the meter (metered fare) and refuse any flat-rate offers, which are almost always overpriced.

Travel Time and Cost

The drive time is the same as a private car (50–70 minutes). The metered fare typically ends up between 250 and 400 RMB, including highway tolls (about 15 RMB). At night, a surcharge applies.

Pros and Cons

Pros: No pre-booking needed; available immediately; luggage friendly. Cons: Drivers may refuse to go to Shunde because they prefer shorter city trips; some drivers may try to negotiate a flat fee that is double the meter price; language barrier can lead to misunderstandings about the destination.

Pro Tip

If you take a taxi, have your destination written in Chinese on your phone. Also, take a photo of the license plate and send it to a friend or family member for safety. Avoid taxis that park outside the official queue—they are often unlicensed and charge exorbitant rates.

Option 5: The Scenic Route – Ferry? (Not Recommended)

Some travel blogs mention the possibility of taking a ferry from Guangzhou to Shunde, since both cities are connected by the Pearl River Delta waterways. While romantic in theory, this is not a practical option for airport arrivals. The nearest ferry terminal to the airport is Nansha Port, which is about 90 minutes away by taxi. From there, ferries to Shunde’s Ronggui Port run only a few times a day and take over two hours. Unless you are a hardcore maritime enthusiast with time to kill, skip this.

How to Choose the Right Method for You

Your decision depends on three factors: budget, time of arrival, and luggage load.

If You Arrive During Daytime (6 AM – 9 PM)

  • Solo traveler with light luggage: Take the airport express bus. It is cheap and drops you in central Shunde without hassle.
  • Couple or small group: Consider the high-speed rail option. The transfer at Guangzhou South is manageable, and the train ride is a fun experience.
  • Family with kids or seniors: Book a private car. The extra cost is worth the peace of mind.

If You Arrive Late at Night (After 10 PM)

The airport express bus stops running around 10:30 PM. High-speed trains also stop around 10:00 PM. Your realistic options are: - Didi or private car: This is the most reliable. The airport is still busy at midnight, so drivers are available. - Taxi: Available but be extra vigilant about pricing. Some drivers will quote 500 RMB for the trip; negotiate firmly or walk away.

If You Have Heavy Luggage

Avoid the metro + high-speed rail combination. Carrying large suitcases through metro transfers and up stairs (not all stations have elevators) is exhausting. Stick with the bus or private car.

Local Tips for Navigating Shunde Once You Arrive

Once you reach Shunde, the local transport system is quite different from Guangzhou. There is no metro in Shunde yet (though a line is under construction). Instead, you will rely on: - Taxis: Easily hailed on the street but not always available during rain. - Didi: Works well here, with shorter wait times than in Guangzhou. - Public Buses: Cheap (2 RMB) but routes can be confusing for non-Chinese speakers. Use a map app like Baidu Maps or Amap (Gaode) for real-time bus tracking. - Bike Sharing: Meituan and HelloBike electric scooters are everywhere in the city center. They are perfect for short trips between restaurants and gardens.

A Note on Food Tourism and Transport Timing

Since Shunde is a food paradise, many travelers plan their itinerary around meals. If you are coming specifically for the famous Shunde raw fish or a double-skin milk dessert at a legendary shop like Renxin Old Store or Minxin Old Store, timing matters. Many top restaurants close between 2:00 PM and 5:00 PM for a break, and dinner service often starts at 5:30 PM. If your flight lands at 3:00 PM, taking a private car will get you to Shunde by 4:00 PM, giving you time to check into your hotel and head out for an early dinner. If you take the bus, you might arrive closer to 5:00 PM, which still works but is tighter.

What About the New High-Speed Rail Station at Shunde?

As of 2025, a new station called Shunde North (顺德北站) has opened as part of the Guangzhou-Foshan-Zhaoqing intercity railway. This station is closer to the Beijiao area, which is home to many furniture showrooms. If your trip is focused on furniture shopping, check if your train stops here instead of the main Shunde Station. However, for most tourists heading to the old town or culinary districts, the original Shunde Station or Ronggui Station remains more convenient.

Final Thoughts on the Journey

The route from Guangzhou Airport to Shunde is a microcosm of travel in modern China: a mix of ultra-modern infrastructure, ancient culinary traditions, and a bit of chaos. Whether you choose the budget-friendly bus, the lightning-fast train, or the comfortable private car, each method offers a different perspective on the Pearl River Delta. The bus lets you see the highway landscape of factories and tropical vegetation. The train gives you a glimpse of the region’s impressive engineering. The car offers the luxury of flexibility.

No matter which you choose, the destination is worth the effort. Shunde’s narrow alleys filled with steam from soup kitchens, its quiet gardens where old men play chess under banyan trees, and its restaurants where chefs treat a simple bowl of congee with the reverence of a Michelin-starred dish—all of this awaits you just an hour south of the airport. The transport is merely the prelude to a feast.
